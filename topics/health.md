## Immunizations (IMM-231, California Dept. of Public Health, 6/25; fetched live 2026-09-21)
Source: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/imm-231_1.pdf
- Required for TK/K-12 admission: 4 Polio, 5 DTaP, 3 Hep B, 2 MMR, 2 Varicella (chickenpox).
- Fewer doses can count: 3 polio doses if one was on or after the 4th birthday; 4 DTaP doses if one was on or after the 4th birthday.
- MMR and varicella doses count only if given on or after the 1st birthday. Combination vaccines (e.g. MMRV) count.
- Schools check immunization records for all new students (TK/K-12) before entry.
- Admitted: with records of each required shot, or a permanent medical exemption.
- Conditionally admitted: if the child has started all required vaccines and isn't yet due for the next dose, or has a temporary medical exemption. The school reviews these records at least every 30 days.
- Transfers from another U.S. school without records: may be admitted for up to 30 school days, then excluded until records arrive.
- More info: ShotsForSchool.org (shotsforschool.org)

## Medication rules in detail (district medication forms; read 2026-09-21)
Sources: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4394/parent_authorization_for_medication_administration.pdf (form dated 5.5.2017), https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4394/physicians_statement.pdf (form dated 6.1.2023), https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/medication_policy.pdf (dated 3.24.2017). Spanish versions are duplicates: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/autoriziÃ³n_para_la_administraciÃ³n_de_medicamento.pdf and https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/pÃ³liza_de_medicamentos.pdf
- Short-term medicine (one month or less): fill out the Authorization for Medication Administration form; a Physician's Statement is not required for short-term prescription medicine.
- Over-the-counter medicine: the district policy says all over-the-counter medication requires a doctor's authorization; it must be in the original container, labeled with the student's name.
- Long-term medicine (longer than one month): Authorization form plus the Physician's Statement signed by the doctor; the doctor may fax the Physician's Statement to the school.
- Prescription bottle must be labeled with: student's name, prescribing doctor, medicine name and prescription number, pharmacy, and how much to take and when.
- The parent authorization is valid only for the current school year; all medication forms must be renewed at the beginning of each school year.
- If the doctor's instructions change during the year, turn in a new Physician's Statement and a new parent authorization.
- Only medicine listed on the Physician's Statement may be brought to school.
- You can stop consent at any time by telling the school nurse in writing.
- Self-carry: a doctor can authorize a student to carry and use medicine without supervision only for a rescue inhaler, epinephrine, and emergency medicine for seizures and hypoglycemia.

## Allergy and Anaphylaxis Emergency Plan (form SMUSD 5.5.2020; read 2026-09-21)
Source: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4394/allergy__anaphylaxis_plan_plan_de_alergia_y_anafilaxia.pdf (the SEES Health Office page links this copy)
- Signed by both the child's health care provider and the parent/guardian; reviewed by the school nurse.
- The form records whether the child may carry medicine and may give him/herself medicine (if the child refuses, an adult must give it).
- The provider can check the "Always-Epinephrine" option: give epinephrine right away for ANY symptom after a sting or eating a listed food (advised for schools where a nurse is not always present).
- For a severe reaction the plan says: inject epinephrine, call 911, then call parents.
- Attach a photo of the child to the plan.

## School entry health exam (CHDP form MCFHS-77ES 11/2016; read 2026-09-21)
Source: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4394/school_entry_health_checkup_requirement_bilingual.pdf (English and Spanish on one form)
- California law: every child must have a health checkup within 18 months before first grade or up to 90 days after starting first grade.
- Your doctor fills out the form; you return the completed form to your child's school.
- The checkup includes vision and hearing screening, a blood test for anemia, an oral health screening, and a TB risk assessment.
- Can't pay for the checkup: call County of San Diego Maternal Child and Family Health Services (MCFHS), 619-692-8808, to see if your child qualifies for a no-cost checkup; they also give information on medical and dental insurance.
- A waiver section on the form lets a parent decline the exam; the Spanish side notes the required shots are still needed without the exam.

## Dental check-up / Oral Health Assessment (CDPH form, July 2022; read 2026-09-21)
Source: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4394/koha_assessment_form_508_72022_ada.pdf
- California law (Education Code 49452.8): every child must have a dental check-up by May 31 of his/her first year in public school.
- A California licensed dental professional fills out Section 2; a check-up in the last 12 months counts (ask your dentist to fill out Section 2).
- Can't get a check-up: fill out the separate Waiver of Oral Health Assessment Requirement Form.
- Return the form to the school no later than May 31 of your child's first school year.

## Hot days (district Heat Advisory Operations page, undated; read 2026-09-21)
Source: https://www.smusd.org/heat-advisory-operations-2
- On days of excessive heat or a heat advisory, SMUSD does not close schools; it modifies school operations and activities.
- Each school has air-conditioned classrooms or common spaces.
- Send water: students are encouraged to bring water and stay hydrated before and during outside activities.
- Dress kids in lightweight clothing that follows the district dress code.
- Signs of heat illness listed: muscle cramps, nausea or vomiting, weakness, headache, dizziness, confusion, heavy sweating or hot dry skin.
- For your school's Hot Weather Plan, speak with the principal.

## Class parties and food brought to class (district wellness documents; read 2026-09-21)
Sources: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/wellness_policy.pdf (Board Policy 5030, adopted 11/09/2023), https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/guidelines_for_safe_fo_od_handling.pdf (undated)
- Class parties or celebrations are held after the lunch period when possible; parents donating party snacks are asked to consider nutritional quality.
- Staff are encouraged not to use non-nutritious food as a reward.
- Free drinking water is available in the food service area at meal times.
- Anyone preparing or serving food to students must read and sign the district's Safe Food Handling guidelines; parents turn the signed form in to the teacher.
- The guidelines ask for store- or bakery-bought food where possible, and ingredients included with food brought in, to help identify allergies.
