# Mental health and wellness: crisis lines, counseling help, Care Solace, suicide prevention

If a parent says a child is in danger right now: call 911, or call or text 988. Give the numbers first, then anything else.

## Crisis numbers
Sources: https://www.smusd.org/mental-health-wellness-home (undated), https://suicidepreventionlifeline.org/ (now redirects to 988lifeline.org), https://suicidepreventionlifeline.org/how-we-can-all-prevent-suicide/ (redirects to 988lifeline.org/learn): read 2026-09-21. Handout: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/suicide_awareness_handout_52020.pdf (Rev 5.2020, the district's current parent handout).
- Mental health emergency: call 988 immediately (SMUSD mental health page).
- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call, text or chat 988; available 24/7/365, free and confidential; text and chat also in Spanish.
- Immediate danger: call 911 (district handout).
- San Diego Access and Crisis Line: (888) 724-7240, 24 hours (district handout, 2020).
- Crisis Text Line: text "HELLO" to 741741, 24 hours daily (district handout, 2020).
- Trevor Lifeline: (866) 488-7386, 24 hours (district handout, 2020).
- Teen Line: (800) 852-8336, 6pm-10pm daily; or text "TEEN" to 839863 (district handout, 2020).

## SMUSD "Let's Face It. Together." program
Sources: https://www.smusd.org/mental-health-wellness-home (undated, read 2026-09-21); https://sanmarcosusd8.smartsiteshost.com/124888_3?articleID=25651 (SEES post dated May 16, 2025, stale: use only the how-to).
- A mental health campaign by SMUSD and the City of San Marcos to connect students with support.
- TEXT: students can text SMUSD to 741741 for free, immediate, confidential help; they are referred to a wellness advocate.
- TALK: students can reach out to a counselor or a trusted adult on campus.
- CALL: in a mental health emergency, call 988.
- The program is described as free, confidential and personalized.
- Common struggles it names: academic and social pressure, anxiety and depression, family instability, relationship issues.
- The 2025 SEES post says Wellness Teams were set up across the district.

## Care Solace (finding a therapist or services)
Sources: https://www.caresolace.com/site/sanmarcos (read 2026-09-21); linked from https://www.smusd.org/mental-health-wellness-home
- Care Solace helps SMUSD families find mental health, substance use and social service support; free and confidential, paid for by SMUSD.
- Call (888) 515-0595; text (858) 766-2022; video chat and a provider search are on the website.
- Care Companions are available 24/7, 365 days a year, in 200+ languages.
- It matches you to providers by schedule, insurance, location and needs, and can help book the appointment.
- Also helps with food pantries, housing/shelter, and low-cost or free medical and dental care.
- Care Solace says it does not share user information with the school district.
- Care Solace is not a mental health provider; for emergencies call 911 or 988.

## If you're worried your child is thinking about suicide
Sources: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/suicide_awareness_handout_52020.pdf (Rev 5.2020; same file also at https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/suicide_awareness_handout_52020_1.pdf), https://www.sptsusa.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Talking_to_your_Kids_About_Suicide.pdf (Society for the Prevention of Teen Suicide), linked from https://www.smusd.org/suicide-prevention-resources-2 and https://www.smusd.org/student-wellness-2. Read 2026-09-21.
- Warning signs (F.A.C.T.S.): Feelings (hopelessness, feeling like a burden or trapped), Actions (looking for ways to hurt themselves, giving things away), Changes (withdrawing, mood swings, sleeping/eating changes, sudden improvement after being down), Threats (talking or writing about wanting to die), Situations (loss, break-ups, getting in trouble).
- Other warning signs listed: isolation, easy access to guns or medication, risky behavior, frequent stomachaches, headaches or fatigue.
- What to do (handout): Notice ("I've noticed you have been..."), Care ("You are important to me"), Ask directly ("Are you thinking about ending your life?"), Help ("Let's get some help").
- Asking directly about suicide doesn't put the idea in a child's head (both handouts).
- Don't overreact or underreact; any talk of suicide should be taken seriously.
- Keep medications, sharp objects and firearms out of reach and locked away.
- Pick a good moment to talk, such as a car ride.
