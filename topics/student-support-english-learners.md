## How a child is identified as an English learner (EL Master Plan 2021)
- At first enrollment (TK-12) parents fill out a Home Language Survey. Answer all four questions.
- If a language other than English is spoken at home, the child takes the Initial ELPAC (English Language Proficiency Assessments for California).
- The district ELPAC team gives and scores the Initial ELPAC within 30 days of enrollment; the score report is mailed home, with a copy to the school.
- Result is either Initial Fluent English Proficient (I-FEP) or English Learner (EL). EL students get English Language Development (ELD) instruction at school.
- EL students retake the Summative ELPAC every spring (listening, speaking, reading, writing).
- The district sends parents written notice of: identification, program options, placement, progress expectations, yearly ELPAC and CAASPP scores, and reclassification criteria.
- EL progress is on the regular report card and is discussed at the elementary parent-teacher conference.

## Leaving EL status (reclassification, "RFEP") (EL Master Plan 2021)
- Four criteria: 1) ELPAC results, 2) a basic skills measure (state CAASPP test or local measures such as the Lexile reading test), 3) teacher recommendation, 4) parent consultation.
- After reclassifying, students are monitored for at least four years.
- Students in special education may use alternative reclassification criteria based on their IEP.
- A letter is mailed home when a student meets all reclassification criteria.
- "Long-term English learner": in California schools six years or more without reaching English proficiency.

## Parent rights (EL Master Plan 2021)
- Parents may decline or opt their child out of the district's language program or particular EL services. The district must still provide meaningful instruction until the child is reclassified, and tell the parent if the child isn't progressing.
- Dual language (50/50 English/Spanish) immersion is offered at Twin Oaks Elementary; SMUSD families apply through the district transfer process. (2021 plan listed TK-1st grade; current grades not published.)

## Levels and helping at home (CDE ELD Standards brochure, undated)
- Three English levels: Emerging, Expanding, Bridging.
- Tips: keep speaking your first language with your child; tell stories or read to your child in your first language or in English; visit the library and keep books at home.

## ELAC (English Learner Advisory Committee)
- ELAC is a committee of parents and staff that advises the principal on programs for English learners (EL Master Plan 2021).
- Required at every school with 21 or more English learners (EL Master Plan 2021).
- All parents of English learners can vote for ELAC members; elections are held at the school before October 31 each year; members serve two years (EL Master Plan 2021).
- Each ELAC elects one representative and one alternate to the district committee, DELAC (EL Master Plan 2021).
- Why it matters (SEES page): required by state and federal law; leadership roles for EL parents; helps staff understand language and cultural differences.
- DELAC meets monthly at the SMUSD district office (SEES Resources page, undated).
- The SEES Resources page says ELAC meets four times a year, but that text names "LCM" (another school), so it may be copied: don't quote it as SEES fact.
- SEES's ELAC page is from 2022-23 (agendas 10/18/22 and 12/6/22). The 2026-27 ELAC members and meeting dates are not published.
- The SEES For Families page's "ELAC" link actually goes to the PTO website (https://seespto.org/).

## Translation and interpretation (district T&I page, undated; EL Master Plan 2021)
- SMUSD offers translation (written) and interpretation (spoken) for parents with limited English. District translators and school stipend staff passed an English & Spanish bilingual skills test.
- Schools request translators and interpreters through the district Helpdesk (https://helpdesk.smusd.org/support/home?logged_out=true).
- Trained interpreters can be made available for school and district meetings; schools keep lists of staff interpreters for special education purposes (EL Master Plan 2021).
- Written notices must be translated when 15% or more of a school's students speak one language other than English (EL Master Plan 2021).
