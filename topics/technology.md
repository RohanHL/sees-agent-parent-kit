# Technology: phones, Chromebooks, internet safety, home internet

Chromebook basics for 2026-27 (Stay-at-School, grades, home-use opt-in, Linewize, Qustodio, insurance) are in
facts.md "Chromebooks and online safety". This file adds district rules. Read 2026-09-21.
Main source: 2025-26 Annual Notification Packet, https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/nrk1cr5amkar8npplhf.pdf
(latest posted as of 2026-09-21; Technology Responsible Use Policy in it is "Updated 4/21/25").

## Phones and smartwatches
- Elementary and middle school rule, "Away for the Day": phones, smartwatches and headphones/earbuds must be turned
  off and kept out of sight during the school day, in and out of the classroom.
- Exceptions: an emergency or perceived danger, permission from a teacher or administrator, a doctor's determination,
  or an IEP requirement.
- A device used without permission may be confiscated and held until returned to the student or given to the principal.
- The district is not responsible for a phone that is lost, stolen, or damaged at school.
- To bring an approved personal electronic device on campus, ask the school office for permission.
- SEES handbook reminder (see facts.md): smart watches stay in the backpack during the school day.

## Opt-outs a parent can request (Technology Responsible Use Policy)
- Contact the school site administrator to opt out of any of these:
  - SMUSD giving your child Internet access at school, or your child using the Internet on campus.
  - SMUSD creating and managing third-party accounts for your child (e.g. Google Workspace apps, iReady, Seesaw,
    Nearpod, Magic School AI). Opting out means your child can't do some projects electronically.
  - Your child's name/picture being published in publicly distributed media.
- The 2025-26 policy's opt-out deadline was "before September 15"; check the current deadline with the office.

## What the district's filter and rules do
- All district computers with Internet access have a filter that blocks obscene or harmful content; the policy lists
  pornography, weapons, illegal drugs, gambling and security-risk sites.
- District accounts and files belong to SMUSD and may be monitored; students should expect no privacy on them.
- Students agree not to bully online, not to share passwords except with parents and teacher, not to post personal
  details (last names, phone numbers, addresses, pictures, schedules), and to tell an adult about anything inappropriate.
- Recording audio or video in a classroom needs prior consent from the teacher and the principal.
- Damage: the district may seek payment from parents when a student willfully damages or fails to return district
  property; a voluntary work program is offered if parents can't pay.

## Internet safety at home (packet's advice)
- The district says parents should also monitor Internet use at home and set rules with their children.
- Social media platforms must offer a way to report cyberbullying, even for people without an account.
- Sites the packet recommends: Common Sense Media (commonsensemedia.org), Connect Safely (connectsafely.org),
  NetSmartz (netsmartz.org), FTC Net Cetera (consumer.ftc.gov/features/feature-0002-parents).

## Low-cost home internet (linked from the SEES For Families page)
Sources: https://www.spectrum.com/internet/spectrum-internet-assist?opredirect=browse-content-spectrum-internet-assist
and https://www.att.com/internet/access/ (both vendor pages, read 2026-09-21; prices can change)
- Spectrum Internet Assist: may be $25/mo if you qualify; $15/mo if the household gets the National School Lunch
  Program (NSLP), Community Eligibility Provision (CEP), or SSI. Includes modem, no data caps, no contract.
- Access from AT&T: low-cost home internet for households in a qualifying government program or with income below 200%
  of the federal poverty guideline; price depends on your address. Already approved: call 855.220.5211.
- The SEES page also lists Cox Connect2Compete (not read by us).
