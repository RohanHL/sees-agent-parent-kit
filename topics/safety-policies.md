# Safety, bullying, complaints and Title IX

District (SMUSD) policies that apply at SEES. For emergency pickup basics, see facts.md "Emergency dismissal".
Read 2026-09-21. The Annual Notification Packet is labelled 2025-26 and is still the latest one the district has
posted (checked live 2026-09-21); staff names in it may have changed.

## How to report bullying (what a parent does)
Sources: https://www.smusd.org/bullying-information-2 (page undated),
https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/safeplacetolearnposter_1.pdf (poster, undated),
https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/ap_5131_2_bullying_1.pdf (procedure, reviewed 2012),
https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/bp_5131_2_-bullying_1.pdf (policy, adopted 2012; the packet carries a
newer version, revised 05/09/2024).
- To report an incident or file a complaint: contact a school administrator in the front office right away (SEES office (760) 290-2600).
- A safety concern can also go to the district's Risk Management: (760) 290-2360 or (760) 290-2387, or its online
  form (it may ask for a district login): https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfp2msBhFuGOCl-HJPWdzjVT-m5gnm4Y2gYfor4W5YCBSq62w/formrestricted
  (https://www.smusd.org/report-a-safety-concern, undated, read 2026-09-18).
- A student can report bullying verbally or in writing to a teacher, the principal, or any staff member.
- Reports can be anonymous, but that may make the investigation less effective.
- Staff who get a report must pass it to the principal/designee within 24 hours.
- Cyberbullying: save and print the messages or posts, then tell a teacher, the principal or another employee.
- Bullying Complaint Form (English): https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/smusd_complaint_form_for_bullying.pdf
  It asks for the victim's name, where and when it happened, what happened, and witnesses. You may file anonymously,
  and you are protected against retaliation (report any retaliation to the Principal or Assistant Principal).
- Spanish complaint form: https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/smusd_complaint_form_sp.pdf
- No reprisal against a student who makes a complaint. School staff who witness bullying must step in when it is safe.

## What happens after a report
- If the bullying is based on a protected trait (disability, gender, gender identity or expression, nationality, race
  or ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, or association), the principal has the reporting party fill out a
  bullying incident form, and the investigation should be resolved in 5 school days.
- The investigation interviews the complainant, the accused and witnesses. A counselor may follow up with the victim.
- Not satisfied with the result: talk to the principal first. If you still disagree, the Superintendent or designee
  reviews it at a meeting (you may bring a representative) and decides within five (5) school days.
- Consequences for a student who bullies range from behavior interventions and education up to suspension/expulsion
  or referral to law enforcement.
- 2025-26 packet (policy 5131.2, revised 05/09/2024): if a child has been the victim of bullying, parents may request a
  transfer to another school, and the district shall allow it under its transfer rules.

## Helping your child (California Dept. of Education bullying FAQ)
Source: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ls/ss/se/bullyfaq.asp (page undated, read 2026-09-21)
- Tell your child: it is not their fault, they don't deserve it, and you will work with the school.
- Keep accurate records of incidents and be specific when you talk with staff.
- Meet with the teacher, the principal and/or the school counselor. Report bullying as soon as you know about it.
- Urge children to tell an adult when they are being bullied. Retaliation or using a weapon rarely works and tends to escalate things.

## Discrimination, harassment and the Uniform Complaint Procedure (UCP)
Source: 2025-26 Annual Notification Packet, https://files.smartsites.parentsquare.com/4153/nrk1cr5amkar8npplhf.pdf
- The district prohibits discrimination, harassment, intimidation and bullying based on protected traits, and
  retaliation against anyone who reports it. Students or parents can contact the UCP compliance officer, the principal
  or any staff member, and can ask to meet the compliance officer.
- Deadline to file a discrimination/harassment/bullying complaint: within six months of the incident (or of learning
  the facts); can be extended up to 90 days for good cause on written request.
- Other UCP complaints (e.g. student fees): within one year. A student fee complaint can go to the principal, and can
  be anonymous if it includes evidence.
- UCP complaint form: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IpIZ8fSm3U2p85Pd7nu7ziOzl6AB2UR_/view
- UCP compliance officer (2025-26 packet): Tiffany Campbell, Deputy Superintendent, (760) 752-1231, eva.jenkins@smusd.org (subject line: UCP).
- Appeal: to the California Department of Education, in writing, within 30 calendar days of the district's decision.
- Williams complaints (not enough textbooks, unsafe or dirty facilities, teacher vacancies) are filed with the
  principal; they may be anonymous.
- Statewide support resources: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ls/ss/se/bullyingprev.asp

## Title IX and sexual harassment
Sources: 2025-26 Annual Notification Packet (above); https://www.smusd.org/100948_3 (Title IX page; cites 2015, likely out of date)
- Report sexual harassment to the teacher, the principal, the district Title IX Coordinator, or any school employee.
  Staff must tell the Title IX Coordinator within one school day, even if the family wants it kept confidential.
- Title IX Coordinator: Christi Frias, Director Student Services, 760-752-1220, christine.frias@smusd.org (2025-26 packet,
  the newest source). The district's older Title IX page (cites 2015) names someone else; don't use it (conflict rule: newer official source wins; stricter answer when it affects attendance or safety).
- Federal Office for Civil Rights (packet): (800) 421-3481, OCR@ed.gov. CA Dept. of Education Office of Equal
  Opportunity: (916) 445-9174, oeoinfo@cde.ca.gov.

## Weapons, knives and firearms (2025-26 packet)
- Never send a knife, penknife, gun or imitation firearm, toy gun, firecrackers, pepper spray, laser pointer, scissors or sharp items to school.
- Parents have sent knives to cut lunch fruit: do the cutting or peeling at home. A child who brings a knife or weapon
  is immediately suspended and may face an expulsion hearing. This applies to all grades, kindergarten through twelfth.
- Safe storage: California can hold an adult criminally liable if a child gets a gun at home. Keep firearms locked
  up or with a locking device, stored separately from ammunition.

## Campus safety procedures and visitors
Sources: https://www.smusd.org/school-safety-2 (procedures undated; page's other items are from 2022);
Room Parent Packet 2026-27 volunteer guidelines, https://docs.google.com/document/d/10fVgGY3tMrWU5ChobG-KcBybnj_8i9NqtLjq3I8X0pE/edit
- For every campus safety incident, including rumors or potential threats, the district assesses, secures students
  and staff, calls emergency services, and communicates to families as soon as possible.
- Visitors and volunteers sign in at the front office with a valid photo ID.
- Siblings and younger children can't come to class parties or volunteer shifts (district safety policy).
- Volunteers may not take pictures of students on campus or post them on social media.
- Volunteers don't join their child at the lunch tables; to eat with your child, check them out and eat off campus.
- Recording in a classroom needs prior consent of the teacher and the principal (2025-26 packet).
