## CalFresh (food benefits) and SUN Bucks (summer food money) (belongs with food)
Source: https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/hhsa/programs/ssp/food_stamps/ (County of San Diego page), read 2026-09-21.
- CalFresh (SNAP / food stamps) gives low income households monthly benefits on an EBT card to buy food at grocery stores, online stores and farmers markets.
- Eligibility and the benefit amount depend on household size, income and expenses.
- Apply for CalFresh and you may be eligible to receive benefits in as few as 3 days.
- Need food right away: San Diego Food Bank, Feeding San Diego, or call 211 San Diego.
- SUN Bucks (Summer EBT): each school-aged child in the household gets a SUN Bucks EBT card loaded with $120 in benefits to buy food over summer break.
- SUN Bucks: you do not need to apply if your household already receives CalFresh. Kids with SUN Bucks can still use other summer meal programs.
- County of San Diego general information line: (858) 694-3900
- Note: https://www.getcalfresh.org/ (the other CalFresh link on school pages) did not load on 2026-09-21.

## Talking with kids about school shootings or violence in the news
Sources: SDCOE post "Resources for Educators, Families to Discuss School Shootings" https://www.sdcoe.net/about-sdcoe/news/post/~board/news/post/resources-to-discuss-school-shootings (posted August 1, 2024; advice only, no dates to quote), read 2026-09-21; Common Sense Media https://www.commonsensemedia.org/articles/how-to-talk-to-kids-about-school-shootings (undated), read 2026-09-21.
- SDCOE tips: start the conversation but also listen; use age-appropriate words; model a calm approach; monitor and limit news; tell your kids how much you care.
- SDCOE (from NASP): reassure children they are safe and review safety procedures; return to normal routines as soon as possible; know that sleep difficulties are common.
- National Parent & Youth Helpline (emotional support for parents, from the SDCOE post): 1-855-427-2736 (1-855-4A PARENT), Monday through Friday from 10 a.m. to 7 p.m.
- Common Sense Media, young kids: if they don't know and don't bring it up, you don't have to fill in the blanks; if they do, ask "What did you hear?"; keep it simple ("Someone hurt people"); reassure them they're safe and people are working on the problem.
- Common Sense Media, young kids: avoid showing strong emotions about the news around them; if you are upset, say "I'm upset about what I heard on the news. It's not you."; keep a typical schedule.
- Common Sense Media, older elementary kids: they may hear about it at school first; ask "What did you hear today?" and correct what they got wrong.
- Common Sense Media: encourage kids to follow school safety-drill procedures and stay calm.
