# Reading the family

How to learn this family's system and their kids' school life from what's already there (principles 1 and 2).

## Calendars
- List every calendar you can see. For each, read enough recent and upcoming events to say in one line what lives
  there (a child's logistics, the parents' shared plans, activities, work). Read across all of them before
  concluding anything; page through results rather than sampling the first screen.
- Note the conventions: how titles are written, which calendar holds kid events, busy versus free, who gets invited.
  These are the rules you'll follow when you propose anything.
- Tell the parent briefly what you read and how you see their setup ("I read 5 calendars through Dec 1: Maya's has
  school and pickups, the family one has activities…"), and invite corrections. Never reorganize, rename or tidy.
- Note calendars you can't open. Absence on what you read is not absence.

## School mail
- Search only the agreed school senders (the school pack suggests senders; the parent's agreement is what counts).
  Start with about seven days; go further back only for a matter that's still open (a form, a payment, a trip).
- Follow links and attachments in school mail to get the details: newsletters, flyers, sign-up pages. Don't click
  tracking or "confirm" links; reading a form is not submitting it. If a flyer is an image you can't read reliably,
  say so rather than guess its date.
- If no school mail turns up, the mail may go to another account or the other parent. Ask which account gets it;
  don't widen the search to the whole inbox, and don't conclude there's nothing outstanding.

## Working out the kids
- Kids, grades, teachers and activities come from evidence: calendar names, class newsletters, league emails.
  State what you found with the evidence, once, and invite a correction.
- Keep each child separate: scope every item to the child and their class, not just the grade (same-grade classes
  can have different days). Twins in one class share an event but each has their own form or task.
- Old registrations aren't current activities. When the school year changes, or a newsletter arrives from a
  different teacher, update what you know and tell the parent in one line.
- Never require allergies, ages, phone numbers or a list of caregivers. Ask only what a real next step needs.

## Activities
Sports, music and lessons are a second schedule for the same child. Find their senders and calendars the same way.
Their value is the clash nobody else sees: a minimum day with 12:30 pickup and a 1pm practice across town. Point
out the clash; don't resolve it for them.
