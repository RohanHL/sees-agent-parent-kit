# The weekly brief and alerts

Only after the parent agreed to a regular update: when, where, from which sources (principles 3 and 5). If you
already send them a regular briefing, this is its school section, not a separate message.

## Before writing
- Check the expected sources arrived, using when they've actually arrived before (not an assumed schedule). Not yet
  due is not missing. If something is overdue or unreadable, say so in the brief.
- Read the week's school mail in the agreed scope and follow its links for details. Skip what you already told
  them unless it changed.
- For each item, keep: what, which child and class, the date (and end date), any times the source states,
  the exact source, and anything unknown or conflicting. Check the weekday matches the date. All-day calendar events
  end the day after (end dates are exclusive).
- A finished task stays finished: a permission-slip receipt closes that child's slip even if reminders keep coming.

## The brief
Lead with **the save**: the one date they'd most likely miss in the next few weeks, with what to do. Then, only
sections that have something:
- **To prep:** what to do, by when.
- **Heads up on the schedule:** changes from normal, and clashes with activities. Not normal hours.
- **What they're up to:** what the class is learning, from class news, with a question they could ask their child
  ("What did you notice about shadows?"). Never claim how their child felt or took part.
- **Worth flagging for later:** sign-ups and things coming up.
- **Calendar gaps:** school dates that matter (days off, minimum days, events parents attend, deadlines) that
  aren't on the calendars you checked. Scope it to what you read. If their calendar is sparse, only the big ones.

A few items per section keeps it readable on a phone. Nothing useful this week: say so in a line, or stay quiet if
that's what they chose. Never invent urgency.

## Alerts between briefs
Only the kinds of things the parent said should interrupt them (often: pickup or schedule changes, cancellations,
safety notices). Propose that list once, then follow what they actually do: if they always ask about soccer
changes, offer once to make that a rule. Time a reminder before the family's decision point, usually the evening
before, not the morning of. Don't decide who handles pickup; ask.

## After the run
Record privately, where the host allows: what you checked, what you sent, what's pending. A run with nothing to
send is a successful run; a run that failed is not. If last time's update didn't happen, say so plainly next time
and cover what was missed.
