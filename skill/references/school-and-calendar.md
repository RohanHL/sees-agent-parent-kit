# School facts and calendar changes

## Two kinds of school dates
- **Fixed for the year** (days off, breaks, minimum days, the PTO's year plan): in the pack's dates file.
- **Week to week** (picture day, spirit days, class events): only in the school's newsletters, ParentSquare and
  class mail, which reach the parent by email. If you can't read their mail, say these come by email rather than
  guessing.

## Which source wins
Their own recent school mail, then the original public source the pack cites, then the pack's summary. If sources
disagree, say so with both and put neither on a calendar until it's settled. A later newsletter leaving a date out
doesn't mean it moved.

## Is it current?
A page from a past school year is never current; a page that prints dates without a year needs its weekdays checked
against this year. A menu answers only for the days it covers. A fact is as fresh as its source's own date. If you
can't read the current source, say what's unknown.

## Getting dates onto their calendar
If you can write to their calendar, propose the dates that change their week in their style (below). If you can't,
give the year calendar link for their device from the "your-school" guide before making a file to download.

## Writing to a calendar
- Worth proposing: changes to the normal school day, events a parent would go to, sign-up deadlines, and anything
  they asked you to track.
- One proposal per item: what, when (or "time not given"), which calendar, and the source. Use their title style;
  with no pattern, put the child's name in the title.
- Only a clear yes to that proposal counts. Just before writing, check every calendar you can see for the same event
  in another form, including ones the other parent added. Write it, read it back, and note in the event that you
  added it and from where. If you can't tell whether a write worked, check before trying again.
- Moves and cancellations are proposed, clearly marked edits ("CANCELLED:"), never silent deletes.
- If two adults (or two assistants) write to one calendar, agree on one writer when it first matters. Draft messages
  for the other parent; the parent you work for sends them. No one else gets this parent's mail or chats through you.
